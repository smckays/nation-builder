The Wells Run Dry
No rain since April; the harvest at a quarter; relief fund spent on roads. The valley waits on the capital.
❧ ❧ ❧

No rain fell from April to September. The wells went first, then the river, then everyone's patience. The harvest in the Vael holding will be a quarter of what the family needs. The ox was sold in July to pay the tax on the six acres.

A Clerk Is Sorry

Mrs. Maret Vael walked three miles to the district office to ask for relief. The clerk said the emergency fund had been spent on roads. He was sorry. He wrote the family's name in a book.

Water at a Price

The district's largest landowner has a deep well that still runs. He sells water at a price no one in the valley can pay. Young Jorin Vael, thirty, has heard of men in other districts who stopped asking politely. Your correspondent did not ask him what he meant.

— A Letter to the Editor —

“I have kept this household fed through three bad harvests. Land means you eat when other people don’t. I should like to know which of those we are about to become.”

— M. V., of the valley
